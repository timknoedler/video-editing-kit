# Recipe: Ceremony film (full ceremony, 16:9)

Read this before Phase 2 on a full-ceremony film: the whole ceremony from start to finish, cut between the ceremony cameras. It should feel like raw footage, but polished. SKILL.md still applies; for vows audio, kisses and the end card see `wedding-film.md`. The editor's `MY_PREFERENCES.md` overrides both. These are one wedding studio's house rules (first worked through on a 3-camera, 2-lav ceremony in Oct 2026); treat them as strong defaults and ask where the editor's taste differs.

## Shape
- Processional → ceremony → recessional, in real time. Don't cut anything anyone says; trim only dead air before the processional and after the recessional music.
- **Opening:** portraits of the couple first, then the venue, guests arriving and details, then the processional (it flows more smoothly than mixing them), 30-60 s under the ceremony's own live music. End the intro on a shot of the musician synced to the music, then cut to real time. Opening straight on the processional also works; ask.
- End: linger, fade to black, then the studio's end card (the same one as their highlight films).

## Cutting
- Long holds: in one studio's past ceremony films the median shot ran 12-21 s, with holds up to 2-3 min; a single-angle film is fine too.
- **Trims the editor may ask for:** long waits (e.g. for the bride, doors closed), shots of standing guests' heads, a long recessional, an announcement before the processional (go from the intro straight into the first walk-down). End on the couple walking away (a cutaway, with the room audio carrying on), fade to black, end card. Never cut anything anyone says.
- Show whoever is speaking; cut to strong reactions and to the wide for structure (a reading starting, rings, the kiss). Don't cut during the kiss.
- Cover every move, whip pan or focus hunt on the handheld camera with a locked-off angle.
- No music under the ceremony, no slow motion, speed ramps or effects.
- **Before showing any cut, run a shake and crew pass** (an editor's first notes on a v1 were "a lot of shaky shots" and "cut around photographers if possible"): run `bg_shake.py` on every 2 s slice of the timeline (on proxies) and cover anything over ~0.8% with another angle (use 1 s slices to find the exact steady points; keep handheld walk-alongs only when no other angle shows the moment). Then make a contact sheet of the whole cut every 3 s and cut around every photographer, videographer, tripod camera, crossing guest or raised phone; a 120% crop can remove crew gear at a frame edge.
- **Crew in frame is normal at ceremonies** (a second shooter on a tripod, photographers): choose the angle without them where one exists and list the rest in the report.

## Benchmarks
- Measure 2-4 of the editor's past ceremony films (`style_profile.py`, ebur128). One studio's: delivered 1920x1080 full frame (not the letterboxed frame of their highlight films), 10-39 min long, older ones -17 to -23 LUFS integrated; they now deliver at **-16 LUFS**, true peak ≤ -1 dBTP. Confirm the editor's frame size and loudness.

## Workflow that worked (3 cameras + 2 lavs, about 2 h to a verified v1)
- **Sync first, by audio:** extract 16 kHz mono from every camera and recorder and run `audio_sync.py` against the longest-rolling camera (the back wide usually rolls first). Sidecar clocks were off by up to 40 min and one camera was set to another time zone. Check each lav against the nearest camera with a local cross-correlation at several points (3-11 ms of drift is fine).
- **Map the ceremony from levels and transcripts:** a 30 s RMS table per source shows music (processional, recessional) vs speech at a glance. Whisper loops on quiet lavs and room mics ("Thank you" every 30 s, one sentence repeated): use `-mc 0`, a `dynaudnorm`-boosted copy, and windows of a few minutes.
- **Judge a lav by speech-to-noise, never by level:** a lav tucked in the officiant's book recorded at -50 dBFS and was wrongly skipped, so Enhance Speech rebuilt her voice from the groom's lav and it sounded like gibberish. It was the cleanest mic (31 dB SNR). Ask the editor where each lav was, and sync every lav to the others at sample level (one was 56 ms off after a coarse sync).
- **Check left/right of every source:** one musician clip's right channel was 19 dB down and the film sounded left-heavy. Mono-centre one-sided sources.
- **Check who wears which lav from what it hears,** not from the label: the "officiant" lav was the groom's (cued before his walk, a private chat after the recessional). The groom's lav caught the officiant cleanly; the officiant's own lav was 30 dB too quiet.
- **Place cuts from scans, not guesses:** side-by-side frames of all cameras every 10 s for the angle plan, then `cam_scan.py` + `cam_flags.py` on every proxy. Check every chosen window for whips, focus pulls and blockages (a crew monitor in front of the side camera, standing guests hiding the back wide, a photographer in the aisle) and move cuts off them.
- **Intro bed:** a b-roll clip of the live musician gave 38 s of clean music. Portrait clips carry the shooter's directions, so keep them picture only.

## Stacked multicam timeline (how the editor can re-cut by toggling)
- Put the angles on their own tracks (e.g. V1 back wide, V2 side, V3 handheld) and cut every section on **all** tracks, with the angles **above** the chosen one disabled. The visible angle is the top enabled one, so the editor re-cuts by toggling clips instead of rebuilding. Spec: `"track": 1-3` plus `"enabled": false` per b-roll item in `xml_from_spec.py`.
- In Premiere the XML's disabled flag is ignored on import: disable the clips with the bridge after import and verify by rendered frames (see `reference/premiere.md`). For a fade to black at the end, disable the lower angles under the last shot or the dissolve reveals them.
- **Audio stacked the same way:** one track per stem (each lav, room, live musician, added music) that **sum exactly to the delivered mix**: build the full mix, measure its single true-peak limiter gain, and apply that same gain to every stem, so the editor can ride one stem without the others changing. Spec: `audio_tracks` (one track per entry). Premiere imports XML audio as mono; replace each stem with a fresh stereo import.

## Dialogue audio (natural, not processed)
- **Enhance Speech (Adobe Podcast or Premiere's Enhance) on the lav that covers the spoken ceremony,** from the first cut (ask before uploading anything). Mix the speech stem with its reverb/background stems added back, keep a constant room bed underneath, and let the room camera carry what Enhance drops (live music, cheers, phone playback): find seconds where the input is loud but the speech stem is silent.
- **House default settings that sounded natural** (a first chain with Enhance at 65-75%, a 2:1 compressor, a ±8 dB leveller, hard automix switching and a -6 dB room duck was rejected as "too processed"): Enhance ~40-50% (background + reverb stems back at 0.5-0.6), **no compressor**, a leveller of ±4 dB over 6 s, gentle rides only, a constant room bed, and one whole-mix true-peak limiter. Measure "processed" as limiter/compressor activity, automix switches per minute and 2 s-to-2 s background jumps in pauses, and transcribe the result to check every line still reads cleanly.
- **Two lavs:** run Enhance on each, then automix with the speaker detected from the **raw** lavs (Enhance makes far voices loud too); calibrate the threshold on the repeat-after-me vows. Use gain sharing, the other mic never below -6 dB. "Turn down the enhance strength" = add back that mic's own background + reverb stems (~25-35%); don't blend in the raw lav (highs smear).
- **Private whispers and unscripted chatter: raw lav, not Enhance.** Enhance turns murmurs into loud garbled bursts out of near-silence. For those stretches use the nearest raw lav (high-pass only) at the speech stem's gain minus ~3 dB, fade the other lav out, 0.75 s crossfades, and run the true-peak limiter again (laughter transients). Treat any chatter between the officiant's lines as raw-lav territory, not only the spots the editor flagged. Find them with `mumble_scan.py` (enhanced stem loud where the raw lav is quiet) and keep only windows with no words in a word-level transcript of the raw lav.
- **Build the mix outside the editing app** (room camera for music and room sound, the lavs for speech with a high-pass, a ride down at the kiss so the room carries the cheer; vows can run a few dB hot, ride them). Verify on a render from the editing app: stereo side channel, ebur128, and a null test against the mix file.
- **Trims inside live music:** pick the join with `splice_find.py` (chroma + mel match of the 3 s before each side; r > 0.8 at equal levels) and crossfade 1 s equal-power so the harmony carries across. Never crossfade audio that is actually continuous (frame rounding can make a fake join; merge segments within 50 ms).

## Intro music
- **Intro cuts:** check the first and last frames of every intro shot: no kiss on both sides of a cut (end the first before the lean-in, start the next after), no foreground blur or passing arms at a shot edge, no two near-identical setups back to back.
- **Under the live music, if the editor wants more:** find the key and chords from the live music (librosa chroma + Krumhansl profiles, tuning offset with `librosa.estimate_tuning` → `--cents`). Live music can change key between prelude and processional; anything added must be gone before the next piece starts.
- **House default sound** (one studio's taste: ambient worship-style pads with sparse piano; the editor's `MY_PREFERENCES.md` overrides): a **pad as the bed that follows the live music's chords** (`chord_pad.py`, open add9/sus2 voicings), with a little piano movement on top (`piano_sampler.py` if Logic Pro is installed, else `piano_synth.py`) about 1 dB under the pad, the whole bed ~6 dB under the live music. Not a static drone (`pad_synth.py` alone read as "a church organ"), not a sampled-piano lead, not a synth harp. `harp_synth.py` is there if a plucked line must blend with a live harp.
- Put added music on its own track; let it play first and bring the natural audio in very slowly (silent 2 s, squared curve to full at ~20 s), fade it out as real time starts, and re-check true peak.

## Colour across cameras
- **Match cameras with White Balance (Temperature) + Tint only;** leave Saturation at 100 on every clip (per-group saturation was rejected). Tone sliders (Contrast, Blacks) are acceptable.
- **Measure in the graded domain:** with the editor's LUT layer on, measure exported frames as they are; to measure without a render, apply the LUT offline with `lut_apply.py` at the layer's intensity (matched the app within ~3 levels). Calibrate one clip first (how much one Temperature/Tint unit moves the cast, by `export_frame`), target the duration-weighted median of the ceremony clips, then a second pass from rendered frames (single frames are content-biased, e.g. a pink dress). Halve corrections on sky, green-field and warm-lit detail shots.
- **Judge tint by skin, not by white drapes outdoors:** outdoor drapes pick up green/magenta from the field and sky; tinting to neutralise them turned faces green. Match skin a* (Lab, on graded frames) across camera groups first and use neutrals only as a cross-check; move Tint in small steps.
- **Don't lift Highlights or Exposure to match faces in bright, white-draped spaces:** a +30 Highlights / +0.3 Exposure lift clipped 14% of the frame (drapes, overcast sky). Keep Highlights ~0, accept slightly darker faces, and check the clipped share (≥250 of 255) on graded frames: aim under ~5%. Highlight lifts were welcome on sunny or overcast exterior b-roll in the intro.
- Compare against the editor's finished ceremony films (`tone_stats.py`: p2 blacks, face luma, skin saturation).

## Delivery
- One studio delivers ceremony films at **1080p** H.264 ~16 Mbps + AAC 320: export a 4K master from the editing app, then `ffmpeg -i master.mp4 -vf scale=1920:1080:flags=lanczos -c:v libx264 -b:v 16M -c:a copy out.mp4`. Confirm the editor's spec.
